The through-line

01  What retro-futurism is

retro-futurism n.

1. The aesthetic of a future that never arrived. More precisely: the future as imagined by an earlier era, viewed from now, with the gap left visible.

Chrome rockets with fins. Flying cars. The robot maid. A room-sized computer with blinking lights that answers when you speak to it. The machine that makes dinner appear out of nothing. The whole optimistic, streamlined tomorrow that the 1950s and 60s were certain was coming.

02  The two directions

It runs in two directions, and both are useful:

→ The future as seen from the past

Raygun Gothic, the Jetsons, Tomorrowland. Someone in 1962 drawing what 2025 would look like.

← The past as seen from the future

Steampunk, dieselpunk, and best of all cassette futurism: the look of Alien and Blade Runner, where starships run on chunky amber CRTs and physical switches. Technology advanced, but nobody ever got around to inventing the flat panel.

03  The essential ingredient

The essential ingredient is the mismatch. Retro-futurism is nostalgia pointed forward, which is a strange and lovely thing to feel. We got the future. It just has worse typography than promised, and nothing has fins.

04  What a retro-futurist experiment is

It is building the thing the old future promised, for real, now that the parts are cheap.

Which is what this lab is, project by project:

  • Model rocketsTuesday Drockets The single most 1950s object in existence.
  • Scale 4x4 trucksY3K RC
  • 3D printingABP 3D Literally the Replicator, the machine that makes objects out of nothing, sitting on a desk.
  • Solar StrategyField work The house runs on the sun and decides for itself when to charge the battery: the house that thinks for itself, finally built.
  • A browser remake of a 1987 tank gameBolo2K Retro content on a platform its authors could not have imagined.
  • An entire toolkit devoted to making a terminal beautifulPhos4 included Choosing to perfect the glowing text-grid instead of replacing it is cassette futurism as a design philosophy.

05  The name

“Built for the year 3000” does the same work in the name itself. Y2K was a real, grubby, deeply unglamorous panic about two-digit dates. Borrowing its shape and aiming it a thousand years out is exactly the move: the voice of a brochure promising a future nobody can be held to.
